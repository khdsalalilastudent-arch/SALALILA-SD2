Ada Lovelace

Software Engineer

Ada Lovelace was a 19th-century mathematician often recognized as the worlds first computer programmer. As the daughter of the famous poet Lord Byron, she pursued a "poetical science" that combined rigorous logic with creative intuition. Her most significant work involved Charles Babbages Analytical Engine, an early design for a mechanical computer. While Babbage saw a machine that calculated numbers, Lovelace envisioned a device capable of manipulating any form of information, such as music or graphics. In 1843, she published a set of notes that included a detailed method for calculating Bernoulli numbers, which is now considered the first complex computer algorithm. Because she saw the potential for computers to go beyond simple math, she is hailed as a computing visionary whose ideas were a century ahead of their time. Today, her legacy lives on through Ada Lovelace Day, which celebrates womens achievements in science, technology, engineering, and math.

Note G

Ada Lovelace’s "Note G," published in 1843, is considered the first computer algorithm, designed to compute Bernoulli numbers on Charles Babbage’s Analytical Engine. It is a highly detailed, 25-step, multi-loop program demonstrating that machines could manipulate symbols rather than just numbers, proving they could handle complex, non-numerical tasks.
